Story 1

If machines can pass the test, what should we teach?

From two studies in the Australasian Journal of Educational Technology (2023): Race with the machines and Digital transformation in engineering education.

Bloom’s taxonomy of learning objectives, from remembering (base) to creating (top). Shading summarises the paper’s findings; it is not a score.

  1. 1

    A test built on Bloom’s taxonomy

    We designed authentic economics assessments that climb Bloom’s six levels, from remembering facts to creating something new, and set them for ChatGPT-4, ChatGPT-3.5, Google Bard and Microsoft Bing.

  2. 2

    Strong at the base

    The tools performed very well at the lower levels: remembering, understanding and applying. They also did better on numeric questions than on text-based ones.

  3. 3

    Weakest at the top

    Performance stayed decent at the higher levels, but “create” was the weakest. The tools struggled to build arguments on theoretical frameworks, keep several arguments coherent, and give appropriate references.

  4. 4

    Learning with AI, by design

    In an engineering course, we built an AI-assisted learning flow for learners and lecturers, using ChatGPT to test exercises, tutorials and assessment questions, with a self-rating system for its answers. It has significant potential, but students need training and guidance to use it well.

  5. 5

    What it means

    Universities should redesign course outcomes and assessment around higher-order skills, especially creating, and teach students to work with AI rather than around it.

Story 2

Finance as open infrastructure: who it can empower, and what it takes

Drawing on papers in the Journal of Risk and Financial Management, Global Finance Journal, Journal of Industrial and Business Economics and Journal of Financial Stability, and a report for the Ethereum Foundation.

Digital financial infrastructure, layer by layer.

  1. 1

    An institution anyone can use

    Blockchain can be understood as an economic and financial institution: a place to make contracts, hold money and use financial services without a gatekeeper. It is open enough that even autonomous AI agents could transact on people's behalf.

    Journal of Risk and Financial Management, 2024
  2. 2

    Digital dollars on top

    Stablecoins bring a stable unit of account to this open network. But their stability is connected: instability in USDT and USDC spreads to other stablecoins, so confidence in the whole layer rests on the largest issuers.

    Journal of Industrial and Business Economics, 2023
  3. 3

    Lending without a bank

    Decentralised lending lets anyone with a wallet lend or borrow. Yet liquidity is interconnected across platforms, and funding liquidity risk, the on-chain version of a bank run, depends on protocol design such as interest-rate rules.

    Global Finance Journal, 2025
  4. 4

    Connected to the world economy

    Open access also means open exposure. Crypto returns rise by about 12 percentage points in months of abundant U.S. liquidity, and rising policy uncertainty cuts that premium roughly in half.

    Journal of Financial Stability, 2026
  5. 5

    Designing for empowerment

    For this infrastructure to widen financial inclusion rather than risk, it needs sound monetary design, stable money, resilient lending and accountable governance. These are the questions my research asks, from programmable money to AI agents that transact on our behalf.

2026Journal of Financial Stability

Liquidity fuels crypto; uncertainty cools itU.S. excess liquidity lifts monthly crypto returns by about 12 percentage points; rising policy uncertainty cuts that premium roughly in half

Excess liquidity, cryptocurrency returns, and the moderating role of economic policy uncertainty

Across ten major cryptocurrencies from 2019 to 2024, periods of U.S. excess liquidity coincide with monthly returns about 12 percentage points higher. Rising policy uncertainty cuts that premium roughly in half, and in China it more than offsets it.

Why it matters: regulators can watch stablecoin issuance, leverage and funding conditions to anticipate liquidity-driven speculation.

doi.org/10.1016/j.jfs.2026.101587

2025Global Finance Journal

On-chain bank runswhat drives funding liquidity risk in DeFi lending

The determinants of funding liquidity risk in decentralized lending

Examines how liquidity is interconnected across DeFi lending platforms and identifies what drives their funding liquidity risk, the decentralised equivalent of a bank run.

Why it matters: points protocol designers to the levers, such as interest-rate design, that keep open lending markets stable.

doi.org/10.1016/j.gfj.2024.101055

2023Journal of Industrial and Business Economics

USDT & USDC leadinstability spreads from the two largest stablecoins

Are the stabilities of stablecoins connected?

Among the five largest stablecoins, instability in USDT and USDC significantly drives instability in the others, with weaker evidence for the reverse.

Why it matters: shows users and regulators which coins to watch to protect confidence in digital money.

doi.org/10.1007/s40812-022-00207-3

2026North American Journal of Economics and Finance

Following the crowdherding in crypto around the 2024 U.S. election

Herding behavior of cryptocurrency during the 2024 U.S. presidential election

Examines whether cryptocurrency investors abandoned their own information and followed the crowd around a major political event, building on earlier evidence of herding during the Russia–Ukraine conflict.

Why it matters: shows how political events can turn independent investors into a herd, with consequences for market stability.

doi.org/10.1016/j.najef.2026.102663

Monetary policy in the age of cryptocurrencies

Reviews the history of monetary technologies from commodity to fiat money, assesses the challenges and opportunities of adopting programmatic cryptocurrencies as legal tender, and compares proof-of-work and proof-of-stake issuance.

Why it matters: asks how new forms of money would affect growth, inflation and the concentration of wealth.

Decoding market sentiment: the power of ChatGPT in explaining Bitcoin returns from X data

Sentiment indicators built with ChatGPT from Bitcoin-industry posts on X significantly explain Bitcoin returns, even after controlling for established sentiment measures.

Why it matters: everyday AI tools can help financial professionals detect signals that earlier indicators missed.

doi.org/10.1108/cfri-05-2024-0278

2024Journal of Risk and Financial Management

AI agents need institutionsblockchain can let them transact on our behalf

Blockchain: the economic and financial institution for autonomous AI?

AI agents can research and decide, but cannot act on their decisions because they lack access to contracts, financial services and marketplaces. Viewed as an institutional technology, blockchain can provide digital-native institutions that let them do so.

Why it matters: agents that can transact on our behalf could greatly augment human productivity.

doi.org/10.3390/jrfm17020054

Revisiting the determinants of cryptocurrency excess return: does scarcity matter?

Programmed scarcity is a major determinant of excess returns for proof-of-work cryptocurrencies.

Why it matters: gives investors an economic basis, rather than hype, for understanding digital-asset value.

doi.org/10.1016/j.iref.2024.103733

2020Real Estate Economics

2× explanatory powerfor housing prices from a real-estate-specific uncertainty measure

A new measure of real estate uncertainty shocks

Constructs an uncertainty measure specific to real estate. It explains about twice as much of the variation in housing prices and starts as a leading macroeconomic uncertainty measure, and it affects prices as well as construction.

Why it matters: better measurement of uncertainty helps households, developers and policymakers time major decisions.

Why it matters: assessment design can develop leadership, a skill AI cannot supply.
